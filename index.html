I’m a Python full-stack developer based in Bengaluru, Karnataka. I trained as an Electronics & Communication engineer — which is why I’m just as comfortable wiring an ESP8266 to an RF module as I am wiring a Django API to a payment gateway.

Today I co-run CampusProjects.tech, a marketplace for ready-to-run, interview-ready student projects. I built its REST API, the Razorpay payment flow, the React frontend and the deployment — and I handle the SEO and analytics that help it grow.

    Research · IoT · ICARES 2025

    Smart-Watch Navigation System
    for visually impaired people

    Read the publication

    A wearable that receives traffic-signal states over an RF transmitter–receiver link and tells a visually impaired pedestrian when it’s safe to cross — through a distinct vibration pattern for each state, plus voice alerts in English, Hindi and Kannada.

    1 Pick a signal state

    2 Choose the alert language

    A browser simulation with illustrative vibration patterns and phrasing — not the device firmware.

    Voice alert

    Signal is red. Please wait.

    Vibration pattern one long pulse

    On a phone, tapping a state also triggers real vibration.

    • Haptic signal states

      Red, yellow and green each get their own vibration pattern so the wearer knows when it is safe to cross.

    • Multilingual voice

      A DF Player Mini plays alerts in English, Hindi and Kannada — selectable by the user.

    • RF link

      Signal states travel from a transmitter at the crossing to a receiver in the wristband.

    • Wearable build

      An ESP8266 drives everything from a rechargeable battery, packaged in a wristband.
